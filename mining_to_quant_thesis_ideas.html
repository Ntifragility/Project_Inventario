Central strategy

Transfer the technical core—not the mining model

The thesis should solve an authentic mining problem while building methods that can later be reapplied to markets: non-stationary time series, streaming computation, rare-event detection, uncertainty, constrained optimization, and rigorous temporal evaluation.

A bearing classifier will not become a trading signal, and a haul-truck policy will not become an execution algorithm. What transfers is the ability to model changing regimes, prevent time leakage, quantify tail risk, process events with low latency, and test decisions out of sample.

Important positioning choice Keep “quantitative trading” out of the formal thesis title unless the university explicitly supports an interdisciplinary thesis. Put the finance connection in a portability or future-work chapter and build a separate financial companion project afterward.

First decide which quant transition you want to optimize for

The word quant covers different jobs. The best thesis depends on the destination.

Quant research

Models and evidence

Prioritize statistics, time-series modeling, uncertainty calibration, causal discipline, walk-forward validation, and risk-adjusted evaluation.

Best thesis matches: regime-aware anomaly detection, survival models, risk-aware energy scheduling.

Algorithmic trading

Sequential decisions

Prioritize optimization, event-driven simulation, transaction or switching costs, delayed outcomes, scenario testing, and constrained allocation.

Best thesis matches: stochastic scheduling and carefully scoped offline dispatch learning.

Quant developer / HFT

Systems and latency

Prioritize C/C++, hardware–software co-design, streaming pipelines, memory layout, fixed-point arithmetic, latency distributions, throughput, and jitter.

Best thesis match: FPGA/SoC streaming analytics.

Detailed options

Six defensible thesis directions

1
Best overall balance

Regime-aware anomaly detection for mining motors and drives

Regime-Aware Anomaly Detection for Electromechanical Drives in Mining Applications Using Motor-Current and Vibration Signals

Conveyors, crushers, pumps, fans, and mill auxiliaries operate under changing speed, load, ore characteristics, and environmental conditions. A useful detector must distinguish a legitimate operating change from equipment degradation.

Research question

Can an anomaly detector maintain a controlled false-alarm rate under operating regimes not represented during training?

Methods

  • Three-phase current, vibration, speed, torque, temperature, and load.
  • Regime segmentation using clustering, hidden-state models, or change-point detection.
  • Compare statistical features, isolation forest, autoencoders, and a small temporal model.
  • Condition-aware or conformal thresholds.
  • Optional ARM or FPGA/SoC deployment.

Correct evaluation

  • False alarms per operating hour
  • Precision–recall and detection delay
  • Hold out complete load/speed regimes
  • Latency, memory, and energy
  • Temporal splits—not random row splits

Feasible data path

The Paderborn dataset provides synchronized current and vibration measurements across multiple operating conditions and includes naturally and artificially damaged bearings.

Transfer to quantitative finance
Market-regime identification, volatility/liquidity change detection, online anomaly scoring, distribution-shift monitoring, threshold calibration, walk-forward validation, and low-latency inference.
Do not make it generic: “CNN classifies bearing faults” has limited novelty. The research value should be robustness to regime changes, calibrated alerts, or hardware-aware deployment.
2
Best power-systems route

Distributionally robust scheduling of mining electrical loads

Risk-Aware Scheduling of Energy-Intensive Mining Loads Under Production and Power-Demand Uncertainty

Optimize a shift-level or day-ahead schedule for one subsystem: pumps, conveyors, crushing, a SAG-mill stockpile, or a mine microgrid. This is operations research and power-system scheduling rather than classical feedback control.

Research question

Does stochastic or distributionally robust scheduling outperform deterministic scheduling when load, throughput, renewable generation, ore hardness, or equipment availability differs from forecasts?

Model

  • Quantile forecasts or uncertainty scenarios
  • Mixed-integer scheduling
  • Production and maximum-demand constraints
  • Minimum run/stop times and switching costs
  • CVaR or chance constraints for tail risk

Evaluate

  • Energy cost and peak demand
  • Production shortfall
  • Number of equipment transitions
  • Worst-case and CVaR cost
  • Out-of-sample scenario performance
  • Optimization runtime
Direct quantitative mapping
Electrical capacity ↔ capital allocation · production constraints ↔ exposure limits · equipment switching ↔ transaction costs · demand uncertainty ↔ return/liquidity uncertainty · CVaR of operating cost ↔ CVaR of portfolio loss · rolling schedules ↔ rebalancing/execution schedules.
Scope discipline: model one process and one main uncertainty source. A whole-mine microgrid plus production plant plus vehicles plus renewables is too large for one thesis.
3
Best quant developer / HFT route

FPGA or SoC accelerator for streaming industrial analytics

Hardware–Software Co-Design of a Low-Latency Streaming Analytics Engine for Mining Equipment Condition Monitoring

Build a deterministic streaming pipeline for rolling statistics and a compact anomaly model. Compare a desktop CPU, embedded ARM, and FPGA or heterogeneous SoC using floating-point and fixed-point implementations.

Possible kernels

  • Rolling mean, variance, covariance, RMS, and kurtosis
  • Spectral-band energy and exponentially weighted statistics
  • CUSUM or online change-point scores
  • Small tree ensemble, autoencoder, or temporal CNN

Hardware contribution

  • Streaming windows without complete-window copies
  • Fixed-point word-length selection
  • Latency–accuracy–energy frontier
  • Median, p99, and worst-case latency
  • Throughput, jitter, memory, and FPGA resources
Transfer to quantitative finance
The architecture can later be retargeted to rolling volatility, order-flow imbalance, microprice, moving covariance, and online change scores from market events. The algorithms and data interfaces change, but the systems principles transfer directly.
Avoid scope explosion: do not build a new network stack, SCADA platform, sensor board, FPGA accelerator, and deep model in the same thesis.
4
Strongest mathematical bridge

Survival analysis and temporal point processes for failures

Dynamic Failure-Risk Estimation for Mining Equipment Using Censored Maintenance and Telemetry Data

Estimate a probability distribution for time to failure instead of producing a simple healthy/faulty classification. The practical output is a calibrated probability of failure within future horizons such as 24, 72, or 168 hours.

Methods

  • Weibull and accelerated-failure-time models
  • Cox models with time-varying covariates
  • Random survival forests
  • Competing risks for different failure modes
  • Temporal point processes for failures and repairs

Evaluation

  • Calibration and time-dependent Brier score
  • Concordance and useful warning lead time
  • Expected maintenance or downtime cost
  • Correct handling of censored equipment histories
Transfer to quantitative finance
Default-risk modeling, trade and order-arrival intensity, time-to-liquidity events, threshold-crossing problems, and competing event types.
Data gate: choose this only if you can obtain timestamped operating hours, failures, repairs, and still-operating equipment. Ordinary labeled sensor snapshots are insufficient.
5
Strong power-network fit

Topology-aware anomaly localization in mine distribution networks

Topology-Aware Detection and Localization of Abnormal Events in Mining Electrical Distribution Networks

Represent substations, transformers, feeders, VFDs, and large motors as a graph. Compare independent per-device alarms against models that use the physical network topology.

Possible experiments

  • Faults and protection events
  • Sensor failures and missing measurements
  • Motor starts and legitimate transients
  • Harmonic disturbances and voltage anomalies

Implementation path

  • OpenDSS, MATLAB/Simulink, or available power software
  • Topology-aware residuals as a strong baseline
  • Graph neural network only if it improves localization
  • Test robustness to topology and sensor changes
Transfer to quantitative finance
Dynamic correlation graphs, cross-sectional anomaly detection, sector or counterparty networks, contagion, and systemic-risk propagation. The transfer is conceptual because financial graph structure is inferred rather than physically fixed.
6
Closest to algorithmic execution · highest risk

Conservative offline learning for haul-truck dispatch

Risk-Constrained Offline Policy Learning for Open-Pit Mine Truck Dispatch Under Equipment and Travel-Time Uncertainty

Use a discrete-event simulator to compare dispatch heuristics, mixed-integer optimization, contextual bandits, and conservative offline reinforcement learning. The system state includes trucks, queues, shovel availability, destinations, travel times, and production targets.

Mining metrics

  • Tonnes moved and production-plan compliance
  • Queue and shovel idle time
  • Fuel or energy proxy
  • Constraint and safety violations
  • Robustness to breakdowns and travel-time shifts

Required baselines

  • Simple dispatch rules
  • Queue-aware heuristic
  • Optimization-based dispatch
  • Behavior-cloned historical policy
  • Only then: conservative offline RL
Transfer to algorithmic trading
Sequential resource allocation, congestion and capacity, uncertain execution times, delayed rewards, policy evaluation, market-impact-like costs, and safe offline decision learning.
Major warning: do not choose this without operational histories or a simulator you can validate. An RL agent that only beats weak rules inside an unrealistic simulator is not convincing research.

Recommended thesis

The best single compromise

Hardware-Aware, Regime-Adaptive Anomaly Detection for Mining Drive Systems Using Motor-Current Signals

This direction fits both computer architecture and electrical engineering, needs little classical control theory, can start with public data, and produces a portfolio that supports either quant research or low-latency engineering.

Core thesis scope

  1. Build a condition-aware anomaly detector.
  2. Use temporally correct validation and withhold full operating regimes.
  3. Calibrate alerts for a target false-alarm rate.
  4. Deploy a compact version on ARM or FPGA/SoC.
  5. Report accuracy, detection delay, latency, memory, and power.

Why it helps the transition

  • Streaming multivariate time series
  • Regime and distribution shifts
  • Rare-event and imbalanced evaluation
  • Online thresholds and change detection
  • Efficient implementation and latency measurement

Alternative: if your strongest potential supervisor works in power-system optimization, choose the risk-aware load-scheduling thesis instead. It is the cleaner route into portfolio optimization, optimal execution, and risk modeling.

Turn the thesis into a quant-ready portfolio

The transition becomes credible when you demonstrate that the abstraction—not just the application—was understood.

Phase 1 · Thesis

Build rigor in mining

  • Use chronological or walk-forward splits.
  • Keep strong statistical and engineering baselines.
  • Measure uncertainty, costs, and failure cases.
  • Write reproducible experiments and tests.
Phase 2 · Companion project

Retarget the core

  • Use one public financial dataset.
  • Reimplement the same regime, optimization, or streaming abstraction.
  • Include fees, slippage, latency, and realistic baselines.
  • Do not claim profitability from a small backtest.
Phase 3 · Interview signal

Explain the transfer

  • Why random validation creates leakage.
  • How regimes invalidate stationary assumptions.
  • Why p99 latency matters more than an average.
  • How constraints and transaction costs alter decisions.

Ideas and practices to avoid

  • Generic LSTM predictive maintenance: using a fashionable architecture is not a research contribution by itself.
  • Random train/test splits: adjacent windows and repeated machine states can leak information across the split.
  • Accuracy-only evaluation: rare failures require precision–recall, false alarms per hour, detection delay, and cost.
  • “Stock-price prediction” added to the thesis: it makes the scope look unfocused and usually introduces weak financial methodology.
  • Deep RL without a validated simulator: the agent may exploit simulation errors instead of solving the industrial problem.
  • A complete mine digital twin: select one decision layer, subsystem, and uncertainty source.
  • No strong baseline: complex models must beat statistical, heuristic, or optimization baselines under fair temporal testing.
